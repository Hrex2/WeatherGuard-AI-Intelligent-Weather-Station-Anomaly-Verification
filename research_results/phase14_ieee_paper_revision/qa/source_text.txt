P1 [paper title] Uncertainty-Aware Event-Level Verification of Weather Station Anomalies Using Model Stability, Temporal Consistency and Spatial Evidence
P5 [Author] Harnoor Singh Thiara[Institution to be confirmed][City, Country]
P11 [Abstract] Abstract—Automated weather-station anomaly screens can identify unusual records, but unusualness alone does not establish sensor failure. This study presents WeatherGuard AI, an uncertainty-aware, event-level framework that keeps model stability (E1), temporal consistency (E2), spatial corroboration (E3), and physical plausibility (E4) as separately interpretable evidence dimensions. We audit a processed five-year-plus weather series, saved prediction artifacts, controlled synthetic experiments, and a multi-phase station-provenance investigation. The processed dataset contains 44,879 rows, with 44,849 feature-ready rows; a chronological split contains 36,434 training and 8,415 test rows separated by a seven-day gap. E1 agreement thresholds select 432, 214, 123, 51, and 15 candidate timestamps at 4/12 through 12/12 agreement. A corrected synthetic campaign spans five selected contexts and eight injection scenarios; at 8/12 agreement, scenario response ranges from 0% to 100%. These are controlled response fractions, not field recall. For E3, source-level station comparisons exist, but provider lineage, sensor independence, and independent event adjudication remain insufficient; the event-validation outcome is INSUFFICIENT_INDEPENDENT_DATA. E3 is excluded from classifier voting. A reproducibility audit finds 42 mismatched cells among 100,980 saved prediction cells, all associated with Isolation Forest, with cause unresolved. Evidence supports candidate screening and an auditable research workflow, not calibrated fault diagnosis or validated operational performance.
P12 [Keywords] Index Terms—Anomaly detection, meteorological quality control, uncertainty, data provenance, station validation, spatial corroboration.
P13 [Heading 1] Introduction
P14 [Body Text] Weather stations provide observations for weather monitoring, climate analysis, and local decision support. Records can contain abrupt spikes, gradual drift, stuck values, missing blocks, and physically implausible combinations. Automated screening is valuable, but a statistical anomaly is only a departure from a learned or specified reference. It is not, by itself, evidence of sensor failure. We adopt the rule ANOMALY ≠ FAULT: a candidate requires contextual evidence and, where evidence is insufficient, the system should abstain from a fault conclusion.
P15 [Body Text] Meteorological quality control combines range, step, persistence, internal-consistency, and spatial checks. Network studies emphasize metadata, instrument exposure, maintenance, calibration, and quality flags alongside algorithms [1]–[4]. Unsupervised methods add scalable screening, but their outputs depend on representation, parameter settings, training period, and contamination by faults [5]–[9]. We assess an evidence workflow that exposes model stability, temporal history, spatial context, and physical plausibility as distinct dimensions rather than claiming a new universally accurate detector.
P16 [Body Text] This repository-grounded study audits saved E1/E2 summaries and synthetic responses, traces the multi-phase E3 provenance investigation, and reports an unresolved reproducibility discrepancy. Findings are separated into artifact-level facts, provisional descriptive evidence, and unresolved questions. Fig. 1 summarizes the evidence flow; Fig. 2 defines E1–E4.

P19 [Heading 1] Related Work
P20 [Body Text] Fiebrich et al. describe mesoscale observation procedures, including limits, temporal behavior, and spatial consistency [1]. Estévez et al. review validation of automatic weather-station measurements and sensor-specific procedures [2]. Network standardization emphasizes metadata and harmonized QC conventions for cross-network comparison [3]. Later work identifies ongoing QA/QC challenges in heterogeneous meteorological time series [4], [10]. These studies motivate treating source lineage and variable semantics as part of the evidence.
P21 [Body Text] Isolation Forest isolates observations through random partitioning [5]; Local Outlier Factor compares local density with neighboring records [6]; and One-Class SVM estimates a boundary around training data [7]. These methods do not estimate fault probability without appropriate labels and calibration. General anomaly and time-series surveys document the dependence of results on task definitions, data, and evaluation protocols [8], [9]. WeatherGuard uses configuration agreement as E1 stability, not calibrated confidence.
P22 [Body Text] Spatial agreement is not a universal truth condition: real gradients, elevation, siting, sensor height, sampling intervals, and shared upstream products affect inter-station comparisons. E3 eligibility therefore depends on source identity, time, variable comparability, and independence. This is a focused narrative review, not a systematic search or meta-analysis.

P59 [Heading 1] Proposed WeatherGuard AI Framework
P60 [Body Text] WeatherGuard treats an anomaly as a candidate event accompanied by evidence records. An evidence ledger stores source, time, variable, quality/comparability, support or conflict status, and missingness. UNAVAILABLE is missing or ineligible evidence; CONFLICTING is disagreement from a comparable source; SUPPORTING is evidence consistent with the stated screen. These states are not diagnoses. Fig. 2 depicts this separation.

P62 [Body Text] Fig. 2. E1–E4 dimensions and interpretation limits. Components remain separate; E3 is outside classifier voting and is not validated for event adjudication here.
P63 [Heading 2] E1: Model Stability
P64 [Body Text] E1 summarizes agreement among 12 configurations: four each for Isolation Forest (IF), Local Outlier Factor (LOF), and One-Class SVM (OCSVM). A timestamp is a candidate when the number of flags meets a selected threshold. Agreement measures sensitivity to these configurations, not probability or independent voting; models share data, features, and timestamps.
P65 [Heading 2] E2: Temporal Consistency
P66 [Body Text] E2 compares candidates with prior same-station history under configurable window, mode, minimum-observation, and deviation settings. SUPPORTING, NO_DETECTED_EVIDENCE, CONFLICTING, and UNAVAILABLE describe screen outcomes. No detected evidence is not evidence of station health; insufficient history is not a negative result.
P67 [Heading 2] E3: Spatial Corroboration
P68 [Body Text] E3 compares eligible observations across stations by variable and time. Eligibility requires lineage, compatible units/report semantics, acceptable timestamp alignment, and adequate independence. Exact and tolerance-window matches are separate. Regional agreement may contextualize a signal; disagreement needs spatial representativeness and measurement uncertainty. E3 records context only and cannot alter classifier flags.
P69 [Heading 2] E4: Physical Plausibility
P70 [Body Text] E4 applies broad bounds, missingness, and selected cross-variable checks. Rules require sensor-, unit-, climate-, and instrument-specific validation. A violation does not distinguish sensor failure from transmission error, conversion error, or rare real conditions.
P71 [Heading 2] Event interpretation and abstention
P72 [Body Text] The event-level unit is a bounded candidate episode, not one flag. The evidence ledger supports review, but no integrated fault threshold is validated. Without independent evidence or adjudicated reference, the result remains uncertain or unadjudicated.
P73 [Heading 1] Methodology and Experimental Design
P74 [Body Text] The analysis was artifact-first: saved CSVs, manifests, reports, source code, and documentation were inspected; figures were generated from saved artifacts. No model was retrained, no weather observations were downloaded for this drafting task, and historical outputs were not overwritten. Results distinguish processed-data description, saved screen output, synthetic response, and provenance/coverage investigation. None yields adjudicated fault performance.
P75 [Heading 2] E1 and E2 analyses
P76 [Body Text] The saved grid contains 12 configurations × 8,415 test timestamps = 100,980 cells. E1 thresholds are 4/12 through 12/12; none was selected using independent labels. The E2 sensitivity file spans 123 candidates, five windows (1, 3, 6, 12, 24 h), three cutoffs (2.5, 3.5, 4.5), and two modes, totaling 3,690 combinations. A separate paired synthetic comparison has 2,880 units per condition.
P77 [Heading 2] Synthetic campaign
P78 [Body Text] Five selected contexts and eight injections—dewpoint inconsistency, gradual drift, intermittent noise, missing block, persistent offset, point drop, point spike, stuck sensor—form 160 context × scenario × injected-time units. We report corrected 8/12 E1 response and between-context ranges. Fixed injections do not estimate field recall or generalize to unobserved stations or fault patterns.
P79 [Heading 2] E3 and E4 evaluation
P80 [Body Text] Exact UTC matches are separated from tolerance matches, and valid-value pairs from common timestamps. Phase 6 comparisons failed the lineage eligibility gate. Phase 9 selected eight events; none was suitable for event validation. Phases 10/11 obtained no provider-ID-confirmed independently adjudicated fault or clean event. E4 uses saved broad physical-screen responses. Parity mismatches are reported without relabeling historical outputs or inferring a cause.

P184 [Heading 2] Prediction parity
P185 [Body Text] The parity investigation finds 42 mismatches among 100,980 cells, all associated with IF: 6, 4, 12, and 20 across its four settings. LOF and OCSVM match. Cause is unresolved; persisted labels are retained. Current evidence does not distinguish stochastic behavior, version changes, missing fitted-state artifacts, or pipeline differences.
P186 [Heading 1] Discussion
P187 [Body Text] The findings show why a detector candidate and its contextual evidence must remain separate. E1 exposes configuration sensitivity but no optimal threshold; E2 depends on history and screen settings; E4 flags missing blocks under the saved broad rules. None supplies independent fault truth. Spatial distance, overlap, or similarity does not prove independent instruments; disagreement does not prove fault because local weather and measurement differences can be real.
P188 [Body Text] E3 has two distinct outcomes: descriptive station comparisons exist and are reportable with units/counts/caveats, but Phase 9 is DESCRIPTIVE_ONLY and latest Phase 10/11 event adjudication is INSUFFICIENT_INDEPENDENT_DATA. E3 must remain outside classifier voting. An interface should distinguish unavailable from conflicting and supporting evidence and never convert missing observations into negative evidence. Current findings support research and human review, not automated maintenance decisions or fault probabilities.
P189 [Heading 1] Limitations and Threats to Validity
P190 [Body Text] Upstream provenance, raw provider payloads, and station-to-sensor identity are incomplete; the legacy retrieval used model-filled defaults. The model target is unusualness, not adjudicated fault. E1/E2 are uncalibrated against independent labels, and configurations share features and data. Synthetic validity is limited to eight patterns, five contexts, and selected magnitudes/timestamps; results are not field precision, recall, or sensitivity.
P191 [Body Text] E3 is limited by uncertain sensor independence, sites, instruments, exposure, history, provider lineage, and semantics. Small pair counts and nonharmonized quality flags further limit inference. Eight events lack independent adjudication; E3 performance is not estimable. Zone radii are provisional. Reproducibility is qualified by the 42/100,980 IF mismatch and incomplete estimator/scaler provenance. Software tests cannot substitute for scientific replication. The latest recorded local suite passed 214 tests and five subtests; a CI missing-pytest failure was patched in workflow dependencies, but post-fix CI success was unavailable at drafting. This is software status only. The literature is narrative, and no prospective deployment, calibration, or operational utility evaluation was performed.
P192 [Heading 1] Conclusion and Future Work
P193 [Body Text] WeatherGuard AI organizes anomaly review around E1 model stability, E2 temporal consistency, E3 spatial context, and E4 physical plausibility. Saved artifacts verify dataset/split counts, screen outcomes, synthetic responses, and the parity discrepancy; they do not establish field fault-detection accuracy. The scientific boundary remains ANOMALY ≠ FAULT. E3 descriptive comparisons exist, but independent provenance and adjudicated references remain insufficient; its event-level decision is INSUFFICIENT_INDEPENDENT_DATA and it remains isolated from classifier voting.
P194 [Body Text] Future work should retrieve traceable observations and report identifiers; verify sensors and station histories; establish coverage, pair validity, and variable semantics; and independently adjudicate events under a prespecified protocol. Only then should E3 and combined evidence be evaluated for fault-level precision, recall, calibration, abstention quality, and utility across stations/seasons. The 42 mismatches must remain visible until resolved.
